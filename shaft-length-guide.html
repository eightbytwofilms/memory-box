The Metal Rod

You don't buy one. It's already attached. · Memory Box build #1

The encoder is the rod. A KY-040 arrives as one piece: a little green circuit board, a metal can, and a 6mm steel shaft sticking out of it. That shaft is the replacement for the old tuner rod. Nothing to buy, nothing to attach, nothing to align. You just have to check that it's long enough — that's the whole question.

1What actually shows up in the box

One part. The shaft, the threads, the switch and the sensor are all the same piece.

Measure yours when it lands. Sellers vary — some KY-040s have a 20mm shaft, some 15mm, a few 13mm. The number that matters is how much shaft sticks out past the threaded collar. Put a ruler on it before you cut any PVC.

2The 15mm has to cover four things

Think of that 15mm as a budget. Four things spend it, and whatever's left over is how much shaft ends up buried in the knob.

With a ⅛" block and a typical thin radio face you land around 7mm in the knob. That's comfortable. And remember you're molding the knob's hole, so you don't have to make the knob's original depth work — you mold it exactly as deep as the leftover shaft.

3A correction to what I told you before

I said "¼" PVC or thicker, cut all three pieces from it." That's wrong for the strip. The threaded collar on the encoder is only 5mm long. A ¼" strip is 6.35mm thick — the threads wouldn't even poke through, so the nut would have nothing to grab. My mistake, and it would have bitten you at assembly.

The strip needs to be about ⅛" (3mm). The blocks can be any thickness you like — they're just spacers. So:

  • If your PVC board is ⅛": perfect as-is. Strip from it, and stack two or three layers for each block.
  • If your board is ¼" or thicker: still usable — just counterbore it. Drill a shallow ⅜"–½" wide dish about 3mm deep on the front face where the encoder hole goes, then drill the 9/32" hole through the middle of that dish. Now there's only ~3mm of material at the hole and the nut catches fine.

4If the shaft turns out too short

Unlikely, but if you measure and the numbers don't work, in order of preference:

  1. Drop the blocks. Screw the strip straight to the back of the radio face. Your old tuning hole is almost certainly wider than 7mm, so the threaded collar just noses into it. Buys you back 3mm instantly.
  2. Mold the knob shallower. Even 4–5mm of epoxy grip holds fine on something that only ever gets gently turned.
  3. Then buy a 6mm rigid shaft coupler plus a short length of 6mm rod (~$8 on Amazon) — a metal sleeve with set screws that joins the encoder shaft to an extension. This is the only situation where you'd actually buy a rod, and I don't expect you'll need it.

If it's too long

Just mold the knob deeper — free. Only cut the shaft as a last resort, and if you do, clamp the shaft itself in a vise, not the encoder body. Twisting force on the can will wreck the sensor inside.

The one measurement to take when the package arrives: lay a ruler alongside the encoder and write down how much shaft sticks out past the threaded collar. Every other decision on this page falls out of that one number. Do it before you cut PVC.
